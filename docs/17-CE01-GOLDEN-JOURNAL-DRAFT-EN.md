# How to Understand the Price of an Original Painting: A First-Time Buyer’s Guide

An artwork price is information, not a score. There is no single fixed formula that explains every original painting. Specialists may consider several concrete details about a work, but the final decision is still personal.

You do not need to understand the whole art market before looking at a painting. Start smaller: understand what is known about the work, what the price can help you place in context, and what the number cannot decide for you.

## What a Price Can Tell You — and What It Cannot

| Read the price alongside | Do not use the price to decide |
|---|---|
| who made the work | whether you are “good at art” |
| material and dimensions | whether you should love the work |
| documented ownership history, when it exists | whether a more expensive work is automatically the right work for you |
| condition and relevant market context | whether you want to live with it |
| practical costs of bringing the work home | your personal taste |

A useful price conversation should make the work clearer, not make the buyer feel smaller.

## What Can Affect the Price of an Original Painting?

There is no universal equation. In professional assessment, several factors may be considered together.

Examples include the artist or maker, the work’s origin and documented history, date, materials, dimensions, rarity or type, subject, condition and comparable sales. The point is not to turn this list into a calculator. It is to understand the kind of context that may matter around a particular work.

One term that often sounds more technical than it needs to is **provenance**. It means the documented ownership or collecting history of a work. When that history is clear, it can help establish what is known about the work. When it is not documented, it should not be replaced with a confident story.

That distinction matters more than memorising art-market vocabulary: ask what is actually known, and keep it separate from what is assumed.

## Does a Higher Price Mean a Better Painting?

A higher price should not be a shortcut for your own judgement.

At MOTGU, we treat price as one piece of information around a work, not as a score for taste. You can understand the details, look at the painting again, and still decide that another work matters more to you.

Take your time. Your first purchase does not need to prove that you understand art. You only need to understand the work well enough to choose it without feeling pushed.

## What Else Should You Check Before Taking a Painting Home?

The artwork price may not be the whole practical cost of bringing a work home.

Depending on the work and destination, bringing a painting home may also involve packaging, shipping and insurance. Oversize works may require a separate quote. The exact amount and process depend on the specific work and destination, so check them when you are considering that work.

If you are visiting Hanoi and thinking about buying a painting, it helps to separate two questions:

1. **What is the current price of the work?**
2. **What would bringing this specific work home involve?**

That second question is practical — and often more useful than another paragraph about the art market.

## Five Questions to Ask Before You Decide

### 1. What exactly is this work?

Start with the basics: artist, year, material and dimensions.

### 2. What helps explain its context?

Look at documented information about the work and, where it is relevant, the kind of market context specialists may consider. Do not turn those details into a formula for the “correct” price.

### 3. What documentation comes with it?

Ask whether there is provenance, condition information, a certificate or other documented history. If something is not documented, it should stay unknown rather than becoming a sales story.

### 4. What will bringing it home involve?

Ask about packaging, shipping, insurance and whether the work needs a separate quote. Keep those practical costs distinct from the artwork price itself.

### 5. Do I actually want to live with it?

This is the part nobody else can answer for you.

Good information can make the decision clearer. It cannot make the personal decision disappear.
