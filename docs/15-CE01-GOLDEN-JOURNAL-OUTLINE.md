## OUTLINE PRINCIPLE

The article must not become:

- a full art-market lesson;
- a list of prestige signals;
- an investment guide;
- a claim that higher price means better art;
- an explanation of MOTGU’s exact pricing formula.

The article should do one small job well:

> help a first-time buyer understand what a displayed artwork price can and cannot tell them, then give them better questions for the actual work in front of them.

---

# SECTION 1 — DIRECT ANSWER EARLY

## Working heading

**A price is information, not a score**

### Purpose

Answer the core question immediately and lower anxiety.

### Main idea

- there is no single fixed formula that explains every artwork price;
- specialists may consider several concrete factors;
- price does not tell the reader whether they personally should love or buy the work.

### Evidence / input refs

- `CLAIM-01` → `SRC-01` + `SRC-02`;
- selected Angle A;
- POS-02 calibration style.

### Writer guard

Do not say:

- price is arbitrary;
- art has no market value;
- price equals quality;
- expensive art is better.

### Reader movement

`confusion → orientation`

---

# SECTION 2 — WHAT CAN AFFECT AN ARTWORK PRICE?

## Working heading

**Look at the factors around the work**

### Purpose

Give enough concrete context to make the number less opaque without pretending to provide a universal equation.

### Main points

Use examples such as:

- artist/maker;
- provenance/history when documented;
- materials;
- dimensions;
- rarity/type/subject where relevant;
- condition;
- comparable sales in specialist assessment.

### Evidence / input refs

- `CLAIM-02` → `SRC-02`;
- `CLAIM-03` → `SRC-03`;
- `CLAIM-01` for the non-formula guard.

### Writer guard

Frame these as **examples of factors specialists may consider**.

Do not imply:

- all factors apply equally to every work;
- one factor mechanically determines price;
- size × time × reputation is a valid formula;
- a specific MOTGU price was calculated from these factors.

### Reader movement

`orientation → useful context`

# SECTION 3 — WHAT THE PRICE CANNOT DECIDE FOR YOU

# SECTION 6 — FIVE QUESTIONS TO ASK BEFORE DECIDING

## Working heading

**Five simple questions before you decide**

### Purpose

Convert the article into something usable in the real buying moment.

### Proposed questions

1. **What exactly do we know about this work?**
2. **Which facts help explain its context or price?**
3. **Is there provenance, condition or other history that is actually documented?**
4. **What would bringing this specific work home involve?**
5. **After I understand the facts, do I still want to live with it?**

### Evidence / input refs

- synthesis from `CLAIM-01` to `CLAIM-05`;
- OriginalityPack `ORIG-01`, `ORIG-02`, `ORIG-04`;
- Angle A reader promise.

### Claim type

Editorial synthesis based on the locked inputs.

### Writer guard

Do not introduce a new factual claim in checklist wording.

### Reader movement

`practical confidence → ready to decide`
